# Squarespace LBO Case Study - Investment Memo

## Transaction

Permira completed its acquisition of Squarespace in October 2024 at a revised $46.50 per-share offer. The transaction was valued at approximately $7.2bn of enterprise value.

## Investment rationale

Squarespace entered the transaction with a recurring-revenue-led platform, more than 4.6 million unique subscriptions, and a record FY2023. Revenue reached $1.0bn, while adjusted EBITDA reached $235.4m, up from $125.1m in FY2021. The historical record supports an investment case based on customer growth, increased monetisation, further margin expansion, and rapid deleveraging.

## Illustrative underwriting

The model uses 5.0x new entry debt / FY2023 adjusted EBITDA, 30.0% legacy shareholder rollover, and a 22.0x exit EBITDA multiple. Revenue grows from $1.0bn in FY2023 to $1.8bn in FY2028. Adjusted EBITDA margin expands from 23.3% to 29.0%, and cash flow repays the new term loan by FY2028.

The base case generates a 1.85x sponsor MOIC and 13.1% sponsor IRR. It does not meet a typical 20% private-equity return target.

## What would improve returns

- Faster subscription and commerce growth than the base case.
- More operating leverage than the 29.0% FY2028 EBITDA margin assumption.
- A higher exit multiple, supported by durable subscription growth and cash generation.
- Greater rollover equity or lower entry equity funding.

The model's IRR sensitivity ranges from 4.5% at an 18.0x exit multiple and a 4.0 percentage-point revenue-growth reduction, to 21.1% at a 26.0x exit multiple and a 4.0 percentage-point revenue-growth increase.

## Key risks

- A 30.6x entry EV / LTM adjusted EBITDA valuation leaves limited room for multiple compression.
- The base case depends on continued revenue growth and EBITDA-margin expansion.
- The actual financing package and ownership rollover were not publicly disclosed in sufficient detail to recreate sponsor economics exactly.

## Interview takeaway

The core point is not that the transaction was unattractive. It is that the publicly visible economics require disciplined assumptions: a high-quality recurring-revenue business can still produce modest returns if the entry valuation is high. The model shows exactly which variables would need to improve to reach a 20% IRR.
